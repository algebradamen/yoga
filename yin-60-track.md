## Yin Yoga – 60‑Minute Track
**Theme:** Grounding · Spinal Hydration · Lower‑Body Release  
**Style:** Classical Yin (passive, supported, still)

**Meridians:** This is a Kidney, Urinary Bladder, Liver and Heart dominant practice, with a strong emphasis on grounding, spinal hydration, nervous system calming, and emotional release.

## Opening Meditation – 3 minutes


**Description:**  
Arrive in stillness. Allow the body to settle and the breath to soften.  
Invite participants to set an intention for the practice — a word, quality, or simple presence.

**Focus:**  
• Breath awareness  
• Grounding  
• Transition from doing to being  

---

## Dangling – 3 minutes  
*(Standing Forward Fold – Yin version)*

**Description:**  
From standing, place feet hip‑width apart. Slowly hinge forward from the hips and allow the upper body to fully release. Let the head and neck hang heavy. Knees may be deeply bent. Hands can rest on blocks, a bolster, or hang freely.

This is a passive shape — allow gravity to create gentle traction through the spine.

**Meridians & Energetic Focus:**  
• Primary: Urinary Bladder, Kidney  
• Secondary: Nervous system (parasympathetic)

**Sensation & Awareness:**  
• Hamstrings, calves, spine, lower back  
• Gentle spinal decompression  
• Quieting of the mind

**Yin Reminder:**  
Discomfort is okay. Pain is a signal to adjust or come out.

### Rebound – 1–2 minutes  
Stand slowly, stacking the spine, or rest in constructive rest.  
Notice warmth, pulsation, or subtle movement along the spine and legs.

---

## Supported Squat (Malasana) – 3 minutes

**Description:**  
Feet wider than hips, toes turning slightly out. Bend knees and lower hips toward the floor. Heels may be supported. Sit on a block or bolster if needed. Elbows may rest inside knees.

Allow the pelvis to sink heavily. No effort to hold posture.

**Meridians & Energetic Focus:**  
• Kidney, Liver, Spleen  
• Secondary: Gallbladder

**Sensation & Awareness:**  
• Ankles, knees, hips, groins  
• Compression through pelvis  
• Grounding and stability

**Yin Reminder:**  
We soften into sensation — we do not force depth.

### Rebound – 1 minute  
Seated stillness or constructive rest.  
Observe sensation moving through legs and pelvis.

## Sphinx – 3 minutes

**Description:**  
Lie on the belly. Place elbows slightly forward of shoulders and rest onto forearms. Allow the belly to soften toward the floor.

**Meridians:**  
• Urinary Bladder, Kidney  
• Secondary: Stomach, Spleen

**Sensation:**  
• Lower back compression  
• Front body opening

**Option:**  
Move elbows further forward to reduce intensity.

### Counterpose – 1 minute  
Rest on belly, head turned right, right knee drawn up.

---

## Seal – 2 minutes

**Description:**  
Turn palms outward, straighten arms, and walk hands slightly toward body. Elbows may lock if comfortable.

**Meridians:**  
• Urinary Bladder, Kidney  
• Secondary: Stomach, Spleen

**Sensation:**  
• Stronger compression in lumbar spine  
• Front torso opening

### Counterpose – 1 minute  
Rest on belly, head turned left, left knee drawn up.

## Reclined Butterfly (Supta Baddha Konasana) – 4 minutes

**Description:**  
Lie on the back with a bolster lengthwise under the spine. Soles of feet together, knees supported.

**Meridians:**  
• Heart, Lung

### Rebound – 1 minute  
Shavasana

---

## Reclined Twist – 3 minutes each side

### Right Twist  
Draw knees in, send right knee left, supported by bolster.  
Left leg relaxed long. Shoulders heavy.

### Left Twist  
Repeat on opposite side.

**Meridians & Energetic Focus:**  
• Gallbladder, Urinary Bladder  
• Secondary: Liver

**Energetic Quality:**  
• Detoxification  
• Emotional unwinding  
• Spinal release

### Rebound – 1 minute each side  
Shavasana

---

## Final Shavasana – 10 minutes

**Description:**  
Total rest. Allow the body to integrate the practice fully.

**Invitation:**  
Feel the echo of the postures.  
Rest deeply. Do nothing.

---

### Yin Teaching Reminders (for cues)

• Find your edge, then soften  
• Stillness over stretch  
• Gravity is the teacher  
• Sensation may change — stay curious  
• Pain is information, not instruction
